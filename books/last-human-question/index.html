The book in five minutes

Arjun has thirteen days: a death in the family, the rites that follow it, and a house full of people who arrive, stay and leave.

Inside him, four voices argue the whole time: the body, the mind, the heart and the soul. They are never named on the page, but you will recognise them, because they argue in you too.

Arjun could be anyone: Meera, John, Kabir, Jane. The rites are the setting, not the subject. The subject is what happens to a person when one of the people who held them disappears, and how the people around them hold them while they learn to stand again.

Key takeaways

  • Grief is not only sadness. It is one ring of your coherence suddenly breaking.
  • Old rites were designed so that a whole community holds the grieving, day by day.
  • Body, mind, heart and soul grieve on different clocks. All four need room.
  • Mortality is the last human question, and nobody answers it alone.

Who it is for

Read this if you have lost someone, or fear losing someone, and want company more than advice.

You may understand a loss of your own differently, or know better how to sit with someone else’s.

The opening

The body noticed first. The tea had gone cold an hour ago and Arjun had not moved. The mind was busy with lists. The heart was somewhere else entirely.

What this book does not claim

It is a story, not a grief manual, and it does not tell you how you should feel.
