The book in five minutes

For most of history, people stayed because they had to. Today almost no one has to stay anywhere: not in a town, a job, a marriage or a friendship. That freedom is real. So is the loneliness that came with it.

No One Has to Stay is the third book of the Coherence sequence. The earlier books look inward, at the people who hold you. This one looks outward: at neighbourhoods, workplaces, technology, rituals and policies, and at how they can make holding possible again for people who are less lucky or less reflective than the reader.

It opens with the author’s own story: a barren piece of land, a few neighbours, and a cricket game that slowly turned strangers into something like a village.

Key takeaways

  • When staying became optional, belonging stopped being automatic. It now has to be designed.
  • Places hold people through repeated, ordinary encounters more than grand gestures.
  • Rituals are infrastructure: they do the holding when individuals cannot.
  • Anyone can start the first ring of a village. It usually begins with one small, regular thing.

Who it is for

Read this if you want to build something that makes people stay: a neighbourhood, a team, a community.

You may start to see your building, office or street as something you can shape, not just somewhere you live.

The opening

There was a barren plot behind our building. Nobody owned it, nobody used it, and nobody thought it could be anything else. Then one Saturday night, a few of us brought a bat.

What this book does not claim

It does not offer a policy blueprint for whole societies. It offers patterns and stories you can test where you are.
