The River and the Salt

A story about learning to think · Venkat Rao · Two Point Singularity Press · Manuscript

A grandmother teaches a child which stories to trust.

The book in five minutes

Aanya walks along the river with her grandmother, Maya. Maya does not lecture. She asks questions, waits, and lets Aanya find things out.

Along the way Aanya picks up small instruments for clear thinking: pausing for four seconds before reacting, checking where a claim really came from, telling what she saw from what she guessed. She learns them without ever being told their names.

Written for readers from about ten years old to their grandparents, it shows coherence at its smallest scale: one person steadying another person’s mind until it can stand on its own.

Key takeaways

  • Clear thinking is learned from someone, not only from books.
  • Pause before you react. Four seconds is often enough.
  • Ask where a claim came from: something you saw, something you inferred, or something someone told you.
  • Good teachers hold a question open long enough for you to answer it.

Who it is for

Read this with a child, or as the grown-up who still wants to learn how to think.

You may find yourself pausing, and asking how you know something, a little more often.

From the book

Maya said it without drama, without the softening that makes admissions easier. Just: wrong. A fact.

What this book does not claim

It is a story, not a textbook of logic.
