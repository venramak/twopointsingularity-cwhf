The book in five minutes

Most books about God ask you to pick a side. This one does not. It describes three Gods: the God humans made, to hold together more people than any village could; the cosmos, vast and indifferent, which does not notice us; and the coherence between people, the God that most directly shapes our days.

The book never diminishes God, and it never asks anyone to give up faith. Its claim is that faith and clarity are two doors, and both lead into the same room: a life held together with others.

It is built as a spiral rather than a straight road, with interludes, and offers two paths through it: one for those who want to keep believing, and one for those who want to see how belief works. Each path is made stronger, not weaker.

Key takeaways

  • God may be the most successful story humans ever told for trusting strangers.
  • There are three Gods worth telling apart: the one we made, the one that does not notice us, and the one between us.
  • You do not have to choose between faith and reason to understand what both are doing.
  • Whatever you believe, the room is the same: people holding each other.

Who it is for

Read this if you believe, or used to, or never did, and still feel something is missing from the argument.

You may find you can talk about God with someone who disagrees with you, and neither of you loses anything.

The opening

There are three Gods in this book. The first was made by us. The second does not know we exist. The third is the one that holds us together, and it has been hiding in plain sight.

What this book does not claim

It does not argue that God does not exist, or that religion is only a social tool.
