The book in five minutes

Every child is born into a village it did not choose: parents, grandparents, neighbours, a whole web of people who hold it before it can hold anything. Nobody calls this coherence. Nobody teaches it. It is simply there.

Then life moves us: education, migration, cities, screens. Each step is progress, and each one quietly thins the web. Because the village was never named, we never noticed it leaving. We just felt the drift.

The Village Came First is the root book of the series. It sits beneath the stories of genes and of civilisation, and asks what made people believe those stories at all. Through a handful of ordinary people, it makes coherence visible, the way nutrition science made food visible without spoiling a single meal.

Key takeaways

  • You were never self-made. You were held into being, and you are still being held.
  • The village was not nostalgia. It was infrastructure: humanity’s original technology for scaling trust.
  • Drift is not failure. It is what intelligence does when it questions what it inherited. Return is a practice.
  • Your Four, your Fifteen and your village are real, and they can be redesigned on purpose.

Who it is for

Read this if you have ever felt surrounded and alone at the same time, and wanted to know why.

You may start to see the people around you less as the backdrop of your life and more as its structure.

The opening

Every child is born into a village it did not choose. Parents, grandparents, a neighbour who always had sugar to spare. Nobody calls this coherence. Nobody teaches it. It is simply there, until one day it is not.

What this book does not claim

It does not claim the old village was better, or that we should go back. It argues that what the village did must now be done on purpose.
