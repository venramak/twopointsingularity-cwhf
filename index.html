What is Two Point Singularity?

Two Point Singularity is the idea that humanity is approaching two turning points, not one. Point One is the technological singularity: machine intelligence passing human intelligence. Point Two is the coherence singularity: humans becoming coherent enough, together, to direct what we have built. The claim is that the second has to come first.

In 3 minutes

Two turning points, and why the order matters.

The name, one word at a time

Two

Because there are two turning points, not one. Most talk about the future names only the first.

Point

A threshold: a moment after which things do not work the way they did before. Like the point where water becomes steam.

Singularity

In mathematics, a point where the usual rules break down. In technology, the moment machine intelligence passes ours and the future becomes hard to predict. Here, the word is used for both turning points.

Point One · the technological singularity

The technological singularity is the idea that machine intelligence will one day pass human intelligence, after which change speeds up beyond our ability to predict it. The mathematician John von Neumann is recorded talking about a coming “singularity” in the 1950s; the writer Vernor Vinge popularised the term in 1993, and Ray Kurzweil in 2005.

Whether and when it happens is debated. This idea does not depend on a date. It only needs machine intelligence to keep growing faster than our ability to agree on how to use it, which is already visible.

Point Two · the coherence singularity

The coherence singularity is the proposed second turning point: the moment human coherence scales up enough for people to direct, together, the intelligence we have built. Coherence is the trust that lets you believe another person: their eyes, their word, their story, before you have time to think it through.

You already live it. The person you would call at 2:40 AM. The colleague who tells you the truth. The neighbours who would turn up uninvited. At the scale of a society, coherence is shared trust, shared stories and the ability to move in one direction.

Unlike the first point, the second does not arrive on its own. Technology improves by itself; coherence has to be built, by people, on purpose.

Why they belong together

  1. Every machine intelligence learns from us: our words, our choices, our ratings of its answers.
  2. What a person judges depends on the state they are in: rested or exhausted, held or alone.
  3. So the coherence of the people teaching the machines becomes part of what the machines learn.

Steps 1 and 2 are well established. Step 3 is the proposition, pre-registered at OSF.IO/W7NYH so it can be tested and proved wrong. How it would be measured →

Three ways to picture it

The engine and the steering

Intelligence is a car's engine, and it gets more powerful every year. Coherence is the steering, the brakes and the agreement about where to go. A faster engine with no steering is not progress.

The cricket team

Eleven brilliant players who do not trust each other lose to eleven ordinary players who do. Talent is intelligence. The partnership is coherence.

The cookie banner

You value your privacy, and you still click “Accept all” when you are tired. What you click and what you value are not the same. Machines learn from the click.

In 15 minutes

Genes, stories, coherence, drift, and four futures.

Where coherence comes from

Genes are why we exist.

Evolution built a body and a brain that could survive.

Stories are how we scaled to civilisations.

Tribes, gods, kingdoms, money and nations let millions of strangers act as one.

Coherence is why we believe the stories.

A story only works if people trust each other enough to believe it together. That trust came first.

Seventy thousand years ago, two hunters walked a forest. One watched left, one watched right, and each trusted the other’s eyes before his own mind had time to think. That trust is older than language. It is the thing every later story needed.

What coherence is not

Not agreement
Coherent people can disagree. Coherence is what lets them keep talking.
Not conformity
Conformity silences difference. Coherence holds difference together.
Not consistency
A person can be perfectly consistent and completely alone.
Not intelligence
Brilliant people can be scattered. Ordinary people can be deeply held.
Not happiness
Coherence is what carries you through the days that are not happy.

The counterforce: drift

Drift is the opposite force: the slow loosening that happens when the people and stories that steady us thin out. It is quiet. Most of us notice it late, as tiredness, distraction or loneliness.

Some drift is natural; it is what a growing mind does when it questions what it inherited. But migration, cities, screens and speed have thinned the old structures that used to hold people without anyone naming them. Because coherence was never taught, we did not notice it leaving.

The good news is the other half of the idea: drift can be noticed, and coherence can be returned to, with other people.

What it asks of us

For you

Notice who holds you and whom you hold. Your coherence is now one of the most valuable things you have.

For families and schools

Children will grow up beside machines that out-think them. Teaching them to hold and be held matters as much as teaching them to calculate.

Where the term came from

  1. 17 Aug 2025
    First formulation

    The whitepaper “Two Point Singularity: A Framework for Human–AGI Co-Alignment”, published on Zenodo by Venkat Rao. It named the two points as the technological singularity and a human, cognitive turning point.

  2. 18 Aug 2025
    First public essays

    “The Two Point Singularity: Rethinking Human–AGI Co-Alignment” on LinkedIn, and a post on LessWrong. These framed alignment as top-down governance plus bottom-up human systems.

  3. 2026
    Sharpened

    The book Two Point Singularity: Coherence Precedes Intelligence names the second point the coherence singularity and adds the CWHF proposal, pre-registered at OSF.IO/W7NYH.

  4. Nov 2026
    Canonical

    The definition on this page. Earlier versions are kept for the record; this is the one to cite.

The words “two-point singularity” also appear in older engineering and physics papers with unrelated technical meanings. In the sense used here, about intelligence and human coherence, the term was coined by Venkat Rao in August 2025.

Objections, answered honestly

Isn't the singularity science fiction?

Maybe the dramatic version is. The idea here needs only something already visible: machine capability growing faster than our ability to agree on how to use it.

Isn't this just Dunbar's number?

It builds on research into human group sizes, including Robin Dunbar's, but asks a different question: what those circles do for us, and what happens to machines trained by people whose circles have thinned.

Isn't coherence just a nicer word for conformity?

No. Conformity removes difference. Coherence is what lets different people trust each other enough to keep working together.

Can coherence be measured?

Partly. The Coherence Lens white paper proposes measures, and the CWHF study is pre-registered so its predictions can be tested. Until results exist, the core claim is a proposition, not a finding.

What would prove this wrong?

If feedback given by exhausted or isolated people turned out to train AI just as well as feedback from steady, well-held people, the link at the centre of the idea would fail. That is what the study is designed to check.

Not to be confused with

The technological singularity on its own
That is Point One. This idea adds a second point and argues about their order.
Coherentism
A theory in philosophy about how beliefs justify each other. Coherencism here is about people holding each other.
Coherence singularities in optics
A technical term in physics. Unrelated.
Venkatesh Rao
The writer behind Ribbonfarm. A different person. This idea is by Venkat Rao.

The Idea

Why humans believe in stories.

Genes explain why we exist. Stories explain how we scaled to civilisations. Neither explains why anyone believes. That is coherence, and it lives in rings of people: the one, the four, the fifteen, the hundred and fifty, and everyone beyond. Choose a ring.

The argument

Five steps, and how sure we are of each.

  1. Established

    Genes are why we exist.

    Evolutionary biology.
  2. Widely held

    Stories are how we scaled to civilisations: tribes, gods, nations, money.

    A view popularised by historians such as Yuval Noah Harari, and still debated.
  3. Proposition

    Coherence is why we believe the stories: trust in another person comes before thinking it through.

    The core claim of the books. Argued, not proved.
  4. Established

    Machines now learn from human words and human judgments, and judgment depends on the state a person is in.

    How today's AI is trained, and decades of research on fatigue, mood and decisions.
  5. Being tested

    So human coherence becomes part of what machines learn. The second singularity has to come first.

    Pre-registered as CWHF, OSF.IO/W7NYH.

Coherence-Weighted Human Feedback

A person nobody is holding answers differently from the same person at their steadiest. Feedback carries the value they hold plus an error that depends on that state. AI training treats the error as zero. CWHF proposes weighting feedback by how held, how coherent, the person was when they gave it.

Research · White paper 3 · The instrument

The signal is not the value.

The Signal Is Not the Value: a first-principles framework for human intelligence in the age of AGI

Coherence Intelligence proposes that AI trained on human feedback inherits a systematic distortion: feedback given under fatigue, overload or isolation can drift away from what people actually value, in the same direction rather than randomly. CI proposes ways to measure that gap. CWHF proposes how to correct for it. Neither has been tested yet.

The example everyone recognises

The privacy paradox.

A person who genuinely values privacy clicks “Accept all cookies” on site after site. Not from ignorance: evaluating every choice costs more attention than they have left. The click is a signal. The value is something else.

The four proposed instruments

Substrate, state, direction, trajectory.

C320The substrate

A proposed profile of 64 skills across four layers (survival, functional, cognitive, meta), scored from 64 to 320. Two people with the same total can be built very differently.

cThe state

A proposed coherence score for a person at the moment they give a signal.

ΔcThe direction

How c changes from one session to the next. The paper's primary metric: is this person steadying or slipping?

Ψ(t)The trajectory

The shape of a person's coherence over time, across daily, weekly and longer rhythms. Same average, different waveform.

These are research instruments, proposed and not yet validated. They are not shown to individuals as personal scores, on this site or in the Neev app.

Why direction matters more than level

Three people, one score.

Three annotators all sit at c = 0.64 today. A is slipping, B is steadying, C is stable. A single snapshot says they are the same. Their trajectories say B’s next rating is likely the most trustworthy, and A’s the least.

Illustration of the paper’s worked example, not real data.

Three mirrors

The gaps between how we are seen.

Self

How you rate yourself.

Peer

How people who know you rate you.

AI

What your behaviour shows.

The paper treats the gaps between these three views as the most telling data of all.

When CI fails

Its limits, on the same page as its claims.

  • Coherent but wrong: a steady person can still hold a mistaken value.
  • Cultural bias: what looks coherent in one culture may not in another.
  • Anchor inflation: people may learn to look steady on the measure.
  • Cold start: a new person has no history to compare against.
  • CI does not measure moral correctness. It measures the state behind a signal, not whether the signal is right.

About · Venkat Rao

It started with a bat, on a barren patch of land.

There was an empty patch of land in our apartment complex. Nobody used it. Three of us started playing cricket on it on Saturday nights. About three years later, around forty people come. We play, we stay on and talk, and the group has its own life between Saturdays.

Cricket was never really the point. It was a reason to keep turning up for each other. Everything on this site is my attempt to explain why that matters, and why it matters more now than it ever has.

Who I am

I’m Venkat Rao. Most people call me Venki. I live in Thane, Maharashtra.

For more than eighteen years I have built enterprise systems, in banking and financial services, manufacturing and IT services, and more recently in education and real estate. My day job is digital transformation.

My training is in psychology. I am a doctoral (DBA) candidate at ESGCI Paris. And I write: nine books under Two Point Singularity Press, three of them out now.

The question that would not leave

The technological singularity, the moment machine intelligence passes ours, is coming. I don’t think it can be stopped. So I kept asking: when machines out-think us, what will humans still have?

My answer is the thing I watched grow on that cricket ground: people who trust each other enough to act as one. I call it coherence. It came before intelligence, and it reaches beyond it.

If the first point is the technological singularity, the second is the coherence singularity. Two Point Singularity is the work of making sure the second comes first.

The mark: a grey point for intelligence, a gold point held inside a ring for coherence, and the line that joins them.

What I believe, and where I might be wrong

I believe

  • Machine intelligence will keep getting stronger, whatever we decide.
  • Every human was held before they could think. Coherence came before intelligence.
  • Coherence is not new. Every tradition has known it, in its own words. Knowledge belongs to everyone, and no tradition owns this.

I might be wrong about

  • Whether coherence can be measured well enough to test. My research says it can. That is still a hypothesis.
  • Whether the state of the people who train AI changes what AI learns. I have pre-registered that claim (OSF.IO/W7NYH) so it can be proved wrong.
  • Anything you can show me evidence against. Tell me, and the correction goes on the record.
