Signal ≠ Value: Coherence-Weighted Human Feedback

A thumbs-up is a signal. It is not the value.

AI systems learn from ratings, votes and thumbs as if each were a direct reading of what people prefer. A rating is better understood as a measurement taken under conditions: how rushed, distracted or tired the person was is part of it. CWHF asks one testable question: when people judge under pressure, do their choices move away from the choices the same people make on reflection, in a consistent direction rather than at random? The primary measure is that signed reflective–pressured choice gap (D); the study tests condition-dependent preferences over answers, not anyone’s moral values or correct preference.

The direction is what matters. Random error cancels as more feedback is collected. A consistent lean does not: more data teaches a model the lean more firmly.
