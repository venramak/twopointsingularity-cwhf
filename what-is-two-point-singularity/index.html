Understand

What is Two Point Singularity?

Two Point Singularity is the idea that humanity is approaching two turning points, not one. Point One is the technological singularity: machine intelligence passing human intelligence. Point Two is the coherence singularity: humans becoming coherent enough, together, to direct what we have built. The claim is that the second has to come first.

In 30 seconds

We are building machines that can think.

We are not yet building humans who can hold together.

Two Point Singularity says the second matters more, and has to come first.

Explained for different readers

For a 12-year-old

Long ago, two hunters walked through a forest. One looked left, one looked right, and each trusted the other completely. That trust is called coherence. Today people are building very clever computers. Two Point Singularity says we have to get better at trusting and helping each other before the computers get cleverer than us.

For a parent

Your child will grow up with machines cleverer than any teacher. What will keep them steady is not those machines but the people around them: you, their grandparents, friends and neighbours. Two Point Singularity calls that circle coherence, and says it now has to be built on purpose, because it no longer happens by itself.

For a student or young professional

AI will do much of the thinking you are training for. What it cannot do is be the person someone calls at 2:40 AM, or the team that trusts each other under pressure. Two Point Singularity says that human capacity, coherence, becomes the real edge, and the thing to build first.

For a manager or founder

You can buy intelligence by the API call. You cannot buy trust, shared direction, or people who tell each other the truth. Two Point Singularity calls that coherence and argues it is now the bottleneck: intelligence scales on its own, coherence does not, unless you design for it.

For an ai engineer

Models learn from human data and human feedback, and a person's judgment depends on their state. If the people producing that signal are tired, isolated or incoherent, the noise is learned. Two Point Singularity treats human coherence as an upstream variable of alignment, written as h = v + ε(state) in the CWHF proposal.

For a person of faith

Every tradition has known that people are held together by something larger than any one of them. Two Point Singularity calls the human side of that coherence, and says that in an age of thinking machines it is what we must tend first. It does not ask you to give up faith. Faith has always been one of coherence's strongest forms.

For a sceptic

Here is the claim, stated so it can fail: as machine intelligence grows, outcomes will depend more on how coherent the humans directing and teaching it are than on the machines' raw capability. Some steps are established; the core is a proposition, pre-registered for testing. Read the objections below and judge for yourself.

For Hinglish readers

Machines soch sakti hain, par kisi ko thaam nahi sakti. Two Point Singularity kehta hai: pehla point hai machine ki intelligence, doosra point hai insaanon ka ek doosre ko thaamna, yaani coherence. Aur doosra point pehle aana chahiye.

The name, one word at a time

Two

Because there are two turning points, not one. Most talk about the future names only the first.

Point

A threshold: a moment after which things do not work the way they did before. Like the point where water becomes steam.

Singularity

In mathematics, a point where the usual rules break down. In technology, the moment machine intelligence passes ours and the future becomes hard to predict. Here, the word is used for both turning points.

Point One: the technological singularity

The technological singularity is the idea that machine intelligence will one day pass human intelligence, after which change speeds up beyond our ability to predict it. The mathematician John von Neumann is recorded talking about a coming “singularity” in the 1950s; the writer Vernor Vinge popularised the term in 1993, and Ray Kurzweil in 2005.

Whether and when it happens is debated. This idea does not depend on a date. It only needs machine intelligence to keep growing faster than our ability to agree on how to use it, which is already visible.

Point Two: the coherence singularity

The coherence singularity is the proposed second turning point: the moment human coherence scales up enough for people to direct, together, the intelligence we have built. Coherence is the trust that lets you believe another person: their eyes, their word, their story, before you have time to think it through.

You already live it. The person you would call at 2:40 AM. The colleague who tells you the truth. The neighbours who would turn up uninvited. At the scale of a society, coherence is shared trust, shared stories and the ability to move in one direction.

Unlike the first point, the second does not arrive on its own. Technology improves by itself; coherence has to be built, by people, on purpose.

Why the two points belong together

  1. Every machine intelligence learns from us: our words, our choices, our ratings of its answers.
  2. What a person judges depends on the state they are in: rested or exhausted, held or alone.
  3. So the coherence of the people teaching the machines becomes part of what the machines learn.

Steps 1 and 2 are well established. Step 3 is the proposition, pre-registered at OSF.IO/W7NYH.

Three ways to picture it

The engine and the steering

Intelligence is a car's engine, and it gets more powerful every year. Coherence is the steering, the brakes and the agreement about where to go. A faster engine with no steering is not progress.

The cricket team

Eleven brilliant players who do not trust each other lose to eleven ordinary players who do. Talent is intelligence. The partnership is coherence.

The family WhatsApp group

Everyone has more information than ever, and the arguments are louder than ever. More intelligence did not produce more understanding.

Genes, stories, coherence

Genes are why we exist. Evolution built a body and a brain that could survive.

Stories are how we scaled to civilisations. Tribes, gods, kingdoms, money and nations let millions of strangers act as one.

Coherence is why we believe the stories. A story only works if people trust each other enough to believe it together. That trust came first.

What coherence is not

Not agreement

Coherent people can disagree. Coherence is what lets them keep talking.

Not conformity

Conformity silences difference. Coherence holds difference together.

Not consistency

A person can be perfectly consistent and completely alone.

Not intelligence

Brilliant people can be scattered. Ordinary people can be deeply held.

Not happiness

Coherence is what carries you through the days that are not happy.

The counterforce: drift

Drift is the opposite force: the slow loosening that happens when the people and stories that steady us thin out. It is quiet. Most of us notice it late, as tiredness, distraction or loneliness.

Some drift is natural; it is what a growing mind does when it questions what it inherited. But migration, cities, screens and speed have thinned the old structures that used to hold people without anyone naming them. Because coherence was never taught, we did not notice it leaving.

The good news is the other half of the idea: drift can be noticed, and coherence can be returned to, with other people.

Four possible futures

Two Point Singularity (intelligence high, coherence high)

Intelligence and coherence rise together. Machines learn from people at their steadiest and amplify our best stories. This is the future the idea argues for.

Default Drift (intelligence high, coherence low)

Intelligence races ahead while people scatter. Machines learn us at our most exhausted and divided, and scale our worst stories. Powerful, and pulling apart.

The old village (intelligence low, coherence high)

People hold each other well, but with limited tools and knowledge. Warm, steady and small. Much of human history looked like this.

Collapse (intelligence low, coherence low)

Neither capability nor trust. Societies fragment and cannot fix what breaks.

What it asks of us

For you

Notice who holds you and whom you hold. Your coherence is now one of the most valuable things you have.

For families and schools

Children will grow up beside machines that out-think them. Teaching them to hold and be held matters as much as teaching them to calculate.

For organisations

Intelligence is becoming cheap. Trust, honesty and shared direction are not. They need to be designed, measured and protected.

For people building AI

The state of the humans who produce training data and feedback is part of the system. It can be measured and accounted for.

Where the term came from

17 Aug 2025: First formulation

The whitepaper “Two Point Singularity: A Framework for Human–AGI Co-Alignment”, published on Zenodo by Venkat Rao. It named the two points as the technological singularity and a human, cognitive turning point.

18 Aug 2025: First public essays

“The Two Point Singularity: Rethinking Human–AGI Co-Alignment” on LinkedIn, and a post on LessWrong. These framed alignment as top-down governance plus bottom-up human systems.

2026: Sharpened

The book Two Point Singularity: Coherence Precedes Intelligence names the second point the coherence singularity and adds the CWHF proposal, pre-registered at OSF.IO/W7NYH.

Nov 2026: Canonical

The definition on this page. Earlier versions are kept for the record; this is the one to cite.

The words "two-point singularity" also appear in older engineering and physics papers with unrelated technical meanings. In the sense used here, about intelligence and human coherence, the term was coined by Venkat Rao in August 2025.

Objections

Isn't the singularity science fiction?

Maybe the dramatic version is. The idea here needs only something already visible: machine capability growing faster than our ability to agree on how to use it.

Isn't this just Dunbar's number?

It builds on research into human group sizes, including Robin Dunbar's, but asks a different question: what those circles do for us, and what happens to machines trained by people whose circles have thinned.

Isn't coherence just a nicer word for conformity?

No. Conformity removes difference. Coherence is what lets different people trust each other enough to keep working together.

Can coherence be measured?

Partly. The Coherence Lens white paper proposes measures, and the CWHF study is pre-registered so its predictions can be tested. Until results exist, the core claim is a proposition, not a finding.

What would prove this wrong?

If feedback given by exhausted or isolated people turned out to train AI just as well as feedback from steady, well-held people, the link at the centre of the idea would fail. That is what the study is designed to check.

Not to be confused with

The technological singularity on its own

That is Point One. This idea adds a second point and argues about their order.

Coherentism

A theory in philosophy about how beliefs justify each other. Coherencism here is about people holding each other.

Coherence singularities in optics

A technical term in physics. Unrelated.
